Resource Center

Understanding
Melasma

Melasma is a complex pigmentation condition that often requires personalized care, lifestyle awareness, and consistent skin management.

This guide covers what melasma is, what triggers it, why it can recur, and why protecting the skin barrier is a central part of long-term management.

01
The Condition

What is Melasma?

Melasma is a common skin pigmentation condition characterized by brown, gray-brown, or uneven patches that usually appear on areas frequently exposed to sunlight. It can vary in severity and may become more noticeable over time without proper management.

Commonly affects

Cheeks Forehead Upper lip Chin Jawline
02
The Triggers

Understanding your triggers

Melasma is influenced by multiple internal and external factors. Understanding these triggers is important for long-term skin management.

01

Sun Exposure

Ultraviolet (UV) exposure is one of the most significant triggers of melasma pigmentation.

02

Heat Exposure

Heat from sunlight, cooking environments, or high temperatures may worsen pigmentation in some individuals.

03

Hormonal Changes

Hormonal fluctuations may contribute to melasma development or worsening.

04

Skin Irritation

Overuse of skincare products or harsh treatments can affect the skin barrier and trigger inflammation.

05

Genetics

Some individuals may have a stronger genetic tendency toward pigmentation disorders.

06

Lifestyle Factors

Stress, sleep quality, and daily habits may influence overall skin condition.

Long-term management

"Melasma is often considered a chronic condition — management focuses on trigger control, skin barrier maintenance, and consistent personalized care."
03
Why It Recurs

Melasma can come back

Melasma may improve and recur over time depending on various triggers and skin responses. Because of this, management usually focuses on long-term skin health, trigger control, consistent skincare habits, personalized treatment planning, and skin barrier maintenance.

A medical-based and personalized approach is important to help manage pigmentation while supporting overall skin condition.

01

Reduce irritation

A healthy skin barrier helps reduce skin sensitivity and protect against external triggers that may worsen pigmentation.

02

Support recovery

Maintaining barrier integrity supports the skin's ability to recover from treatments and daily environmental exposure.

03

Healthier skin

Long-term barrier maintenance helps maintain healthier-looking skin and supports comfort throughout your treatment journey.
